## Initial Capital Costs

The cost of starting your booth will depend on the type of photobooth setup you want and how premium you'd like it to look. Here's a realistic breakdown for 2025:

### 1. Photobooth Hardware (₱70,000–₱200,000)

- DSLR or mirrorless camera (₱30,000–₱80,000)
- Lighting setup (₱10,000–₱25,000)
- Printer for instant prints (₱15,000–₱40,000)
- Booth structure or enclosure (₱15,000–₱40,000)

💡 Budget builds can start with a **tablet/iPad booth**, while premium setups can feature DSLR cameras, mirror booths, or even 360-degree booths.

### 2. Photobooth Software (₱0–₱20,000 annually)

This is the "engine" that runs your booth. Without the right app, you'll struggle with operations.

- **DSLRBooth & LumaBooth** – popular globally, but can feel complicated and pricey.
- **Easy Picsy** – a **Philippine-first photobooth software** that's simple, mobile-ready, and designed for contactless payments (GCash & QRPH).

### 3. Marketing & Branding (₱10,000–₱30,000)

- Create a website or Facebook page
- Invest in props, backdrops, and themed designs
- Professional branding: logo, signage, and event sample shots

💡 **Estimated Startup Total**: ₱90,000–₱250,000, depending on whether you go budget-friendly (iPad setup) or premium DSLR booth.

## Ongoing Expenses

Running a photobooth business in the Philippines has recurring costs, but they're generally manageable and scale with how many events you book.

- **Consumables** (photo paper & ink): ₱3,000–₱8,000/month
- **Transportation** (to and from events): ₱2,000–₱6,000/month
- **Staff (optional):** ₱1,000–₱2,000 per event for an assistant
- **Marketing boosts** (FB ads, promos): ₱2,000–₱5,000/month

💡 Total monthly variable costs usually land between **₱7,000–₱15,000**, making it very sustainable even for part-time operators.
